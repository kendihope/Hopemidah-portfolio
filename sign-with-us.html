Sign With Us

A modern sign language learning platform, designed to make education more accessible, engaging, and scalable.

Landing page — desktop
Overview

Sign With Us pairs a learning management system with a marketing site, built around one goal: making sign language education easier to reach, easier to stick with, and ready to grow. Accessibility wasn't a checklist added at the end — it shaped the interface from the ground up, designed with WCAG guidelines and the needs of D/deaf and hard-of-hearing learners in mind.

Focus areas
Accessibility WCAG Disability-inclusive design Visual identity LMS design Marketing site
Sign-up flow — desktop · Home screen — mobile
The problem

Despite growing demand for inclusive communication, sign language education stays hard to reach for most people. Existing resources tend to be unstructured and inconsistent, expensive or limited, poorly designed for engagement, not built for mobile learning, and missing any real progress tracking or motivation.

On top of that, organizations and institutions often have no clear way to tell whether their own environments are genuinely accessible for people with disabilities.

The solution

Sign With Us was designed as a modern learning ecosystem built from two connected products.

  • 01A high-converting landing website — a clear, professional public site that explains the platform, lays out pricing, encourages donations, and guides people into the learning system.
  • 02A structured learning platform — a web-first LMS built for long-term engagement, supporting free learning, paid tiers, and a curriculum designed to scale.
Key goals
  • Create a simple, intuitive learning experience.
  • Design an app-like interface that works well on any device.
  • Build a clear monetization model — free, pro, and donations.
  • Support payments in both KES and USD.
Who it's for
  • 01Students and beginners
  • 02Professionals — teachers, healthcare workers, customer service teams
  • 03Families supporting deaf or hard-of-hearing individuals
  • 04Institutions seeking inclusive learning resources
  • 05Organizations looking for accessibility audit services
My role

As the sole designer working with two developers, I handled UX strategy and product planning, user flows and information architecture, wireframing and prototyping, UI design and responsive layouts, and the design system itself.

Process
  • 01Discovery & research — clarifying the revenue structure (freemium + donations), benchmarking popular learning platforms, reviewing common engagement problems in online education, and identifying accessibility-first requirements.
  • 02Information architecture — cutting complexity down to one predictable flow: landing page → sign up → learn → practice → track progress → upgrade (optional), designed to feel familiar, like the education apps people already use.
  • 03Wireframing — validating lesson layouts, course browsing, dashboard usability, quiz flow interactions, and mobile-first responsiveness before moving into high-fidelity UI.
  • 04Design system — a reusable system covering typography hierarchy, an accessible color palette, buttons/inputs/cards/navigation components, and a spacing and layout grid — keeping the website and LMS visually consistent as both scale.
UI direction & accessibility
Visual style

Friendly and welcoming, clean and modern, educational and calm, app-like and structured, accessible and readable — the goal throughout was to reduce cognitive load and make learning feel effortless.

Accessibility, built in

Treated as a core requirement and aligned with WCAG-inspired principles: strong color contrast, large tap targets across mobile and web, clear hierarchy and spacing, predictable navigation, short lesson segmentation to reduce overload, and planning for captions, transcripts, and screen-reader support.

Challenges & design solutions
Challenges

Balancing simplicity with scalability — staying simple for beginners while still supporting future growth. Supporting multiple audiences — learners, donors, and organizations each needed their own entry point without cluttering the interface. Creating clear upgrade value — the Pro tier had to feel worthwhile without making free users feel shut out.

Solutions

Modular UI components built for scalability, a clear content hierarchy with simplified navigation, progressive feature unlocking from free to pro, repeated CTAs on the landing page to support conversion, and one unified system spanning both the website and the LMS.

Final outcome
  • A full landing website UI design.
  • A complete LMS user experience.
  • Responsive screens across all breakpoints.
  • A scalable design system shared across both products.
  • A monetization and donation structure built directly into the UX.
  • Accessibility audit services positioned in for future expansion.
Delivered through Figma with full access and handoff documentation.
Key takeaways
  • Learning products need to be designed for motivation, not just information.
  • Accessibility is strongest when it's built into the foundation, not added later.
  • Clear monetization depends on strong value communication.
  • A good design system reduces development friction and speeds up scaling.
